STRUCTURAL ENGINEERING · MATLAB · COMPUTATIONAL MODELING

Truss Structural Design & Analysis

A team-based structural engineering project focused on optimizing a planar truss for load-carrying capacity while satisfying geometric, material, and cost constraints.

Engineering Mechanics MATLAB Group Project

Final cambered truss design developed through iterative computational analysis.

OVERVIEW

The goal of the project was to design a planar truss that maximized load-carrying capacity while remaining within specified geometric, material, and cost constraints.

Because buckling was the dominant failure mode, our team focused on distributing compressive forces effectively, shortening critical compression members, and placing longer members primarily in tension.

DESIGN PROCESS

The final design evolved from two preliminary truss geometries. After comparing their force distributions and structural limitations, we selected one configuration and refined it rather than starting from an entirely new geometry.

We iteratively adjusted joint coordinates and member lengths, running the MATLAB analysis after each modification to evaluate internal forces, buckling behavior, and total material usage.

ANALYSIS

The MATLAB model constructed the truss from its joint coordinates and connection matrix, assembled the global equilibrium system, and solved for member forces and support reactions under the applied load.

Compression members were evaluated using the class buckling relationship from experimental lab data. The model identified the critical compression member and used its buckling capacity to predict the theoretical failure load.

RESULTS

The final design had a theoretical failure load of 34.51 ± 1.58 oz, with member m8 identified as the critical member. Its predicted buckling strength was 20.77 ± 1.58 oz.

The design had a calculated cost of $163.55 and a load-to-cost ratio of 0.211 oz/$.

Because the project required consideration of human-safety scenarios, our team selected a conservative factor of safety of 3.0, resulting in an allowable load of approximately 11.50 oz.

ENGINEERING JUDGMENT

An important part of the project was evaluating the limitations of computational analysis. Our team discussed the risk of relying on software without verifying whether the model and boundary conditions accurately represented the physical structure.

We incorporated this perspective into our design process by emphasizing hand calculations, physical reasoning, and conservative safety assumptions alongside computational results.
