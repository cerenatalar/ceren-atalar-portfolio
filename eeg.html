Brightness and Brainwaves

A collaborative EEG study investigating whether the brightness of visual stimuli affects frontal alpha brainwave activity, replicating and extending findings from previous research.

Systems Physiology Lab Experimental Research Group Project
OVERVIEW

This project investigated the relationship between the brightness of visual stimuli and alpha brainwave activity in the frontal cortex.

Our experiment used EEG recordings to compare brain activity while participants viewed the same set of images under regular and reduced brightness conditions.

EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN

Each participant completed four experimental loops using the same ten images. Two loops used regular brightness and two used reduced brightness, allowing us to compare alpha activity across the two visual conditions.

We initially collected data from 22 participants. After removing outliers, the final analysis included 16 data points (N = 16).

EEG DATA COLLECTION

EEG recordings were collected using the ROAM-EEG system and IX-TA software. We examined alpha activity from the left and right cortical zones during each experimental loop.

The recorded signals were used to calculate maximum minus minimum alpha amplitude for each condition, allowing us to quantify changes in EEG activity associated with stimulus brightness.

DATA ANALYSIS

We averaged the left and right cortical alpha amplitudes across the regular- and reduced-brightness trials and compared the resulting conditions statistically.

A paired t-test was used to evaluate whether brightness produced a statistically significant difference in average alpha amplitude.

RESULTS

The data showed slightly higher average maximum-to-minimum alpha amplitudes under regular brightness compared with reduced brightness.

However, the difference was not statistically significant based on the paired t-test (t15 = 0.0596, p = 0.0683).

These results did not support our original hypothesis that increasing visual brightness would significantly decrease maximum frontal alpha amplitude.

INTERPRETATION

The findings suggest that the relationship between visual brightness and alpha EEG activity may be more dependent on factors such as brain region, stimulus characteristics, and analysis window than initially expected.

We also considered environmental factors such as ambient light, noise, conversation, and participant movement as potential sources of variability in the recordings.

LIMITATIONS & FUTURE WORK

The experiment was limited to frontal EEG recordings, while visual processing effects may be more pronounced in posterior brain regions.

Future experiments could use a more controlled environment, including reduced ambient light and acoustic isolation, to better isolate the effect of stimulus brightness on alpha activity.
