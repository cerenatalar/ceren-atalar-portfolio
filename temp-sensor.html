Room Temperature Monitor

A compact Arduino-based temperature monitoring system that displays real-time readings and provides visual and auditory alerts when the temperature moves outside a defined range.

Hands On Engineering Arduino Individual Project

Final Room Temperature Monitor prototype.

OVERVIEW

The Room Temperature Monitor was designed to continuously measure and display ambient temperature while providing an alert when the reading falls outside a user-defined range.

The system combines a TMP36 analog temperature sensor, Arduino Uno, I2C LCD display, LEDs, and a buzzer within a portable enclosure.

SYSTEM DESIGN

The Arduino functions as the central processing unit, receiving the analog voltage output from the TMP36, converting the measurement into temperature, and controlling the display and alert components.

Temperature is displayed in both Celsius and Fahrenheit. When the reading moves outside the defined 80–90°F range, the system activates an LED and buzzer to provide immediate feedback.

ENCLOSURE & PROTOTYPING

I designed the physical organization of the components within the enclosure, including a custom 3D-printed battery holder and an acrylic mounting plate for the Arduino.

Different wiring techniques were selected based on the requirements of each connection. I used soldering and heat-shrink tubing for smaller components such as the temperature sensor, LEDs, buzzer, and power connections, while spade connectors and jumper wires were used where they provided easier assembly and maintenance.

PROGRAMMING

The Arduino program reads the TMP36's analog voltage, converts it into Celsius and Fahrenheit, updates the LCD display, and continuously checks whether the temperature is within the defined operating range.

The system also controls the LED and buzzer response, allowing the device to function as both a monitoring and alert system.

PERFORMANCE

The completed prototype successfully met the project's functional and safety requirements and demonstrated continuous temperature monitoring with customizable alert behavior.

The system drew approximately 77 mA and was estimated to operate for approximately 6.5 hours from its internal 9V battery. The design also supported external USB-B or 5V power for stationary operation.

DESIGN CONSIDERATIONS

The prototype prioritized functionality, flexibility, and ease of construction rather than the compactness and precision of a commercial thermometer.

The TMP36 has an approximately ±2°C temperature error, and the project did not include formal reliability testing. These limitations were considered when evaluating the appropriate applications for the final design.
